# What a DJ actually does

A DJ set is one long piece of music made from many tracks. Good DJs make the joins disappear, so the crowd never notices one track ending and the next beginning.

## The technique

Five jobs make up DJing:

1. **Selection**: choosing the next track, one that fits the tempo, the key and the mood.
2. **Beatmatching**: getting two tracks to play at the same speed, with their kick drums landing together.
3. **Phrasing**: starting the next track at the right moment, so its sections line up with the current track's.
4. **Blending**: moving smoothly from one track to the other, part by part.
5. **Energy**: shaping the whole set, building up, letting off and peaking at the right time.

## In Touch Jockey

- **Selection** is yours, with help: the library shows each track's tempo and how well it fits.
- **Beatmatching** is done for you by **SYNC**, so you can focus on everything else first.
- **Phrasing** gets a helping hand: the timeline shows phrases, and the incoming track can start exactly on the next one.
- **Blending** starts with the **handoff lanes**: one simple gesture per part of the music.
- **Energy** is all yours.

## Practice

Listen to a DJ mix you like, any house or melodic techno set. Try to catch the moment one track hands over to the next. Notice how long it takes; often it's a minute or more. That slow, smooth handover is what you'll learn first.
