# Your first transition: the handoff

This is the core move of house and techno DJing: hand the music over from one track to the next, **part by part**, over a phrase or two.

## The technique

Music has three main bands:

- **Lows**: kick drum and bass.
- **Mids**: chords, vocals, synths.
- **Highs**: hi-hats, cymbals, shimmer.

DJs blend band by band. The new track's highs and mids come in gradually, then the basslines **swap**: the old track's bass out and the new track's bass in, at the same moment, on a downbeat. Two basslines playing together sound muddy, which is why the bass swap is the moment that matters.

## In Touch Jockey

The phone shows three **handoff lanes**: HIGHS, MIDS and LOWS. Each lane moves one band from deck A to deck B.

1. With deck A playing, tap **Bring in B next phrase**. B starts at the next phrase, in sync and silent.
2. Over the next 8–16 bars, slowly slide **HIGHS**, then **MIDS**, towards B. You'll hear B's hats and melody arrive.
3. On a phrase line, **flick LOWS** towards B. The bass swaps exactly on the next bar.
4. Finish the other lanes. When every lane is on B, B becomes the playing deck and A stops.

> Hold the phone upright and you can slide all three lanes with three fingers.

The playing track is always the **outgoing** deck. If you pause it while the other one plays, the one still playing takes over, every lane moves to it, and **Next phrase** and **Next bar** move to the paused deck, ready to bring it back in.

## Setting up a practice run

In a set, a handoff plays the old track's **outro** over the new track's **intro**. Both are mostly drums, so they blend cleanly. Practise the same way.

**Go practice** sets this up for you. It uses the two tracks on the decks, or picks two from your library with similar tempos and keys that blend. It parks the track you mix out of four phrases before its end, puts the other at bar 1, and both land on phrase lines, so they start in phrase. Press **Play all** and do the handoff.

### Lining up the next track yourself

Press **LINE UP** at the top right of the timeline. It shows both whole tracks, one above the other: anything stacked vertically plays at the same moment.

1. **Drag the waiting track** left or right under the playing one. It snaps to phrase lines, and the gold label says where it starts: "B STARTS ON A'S BAR 97". It can't start in the part of the playing track that has already gone by. Drag it further left and it starts later in its own track instead, skipping the part that would already have played.
2. Put its **intro** under the playing track's **outro**, so its bass comes in as the other's goes.
3. Play. When the playing track reaches that bar, the waiting track **starts by itself**, in sync and silent, ready for the lanes.

To start from a different part of the waiting track, click its deck's overview first. **Next phrase** still starts it straight away instead.

The practice bar has two more buttons:

- **Reset**: after a run, sets the tracks up again for the next one (the other way round, if the handoff swapped them), with the lanes back on the outgoing deck. At the mixer level it resets the mixer too.
- **New pair**: loads two different tracks.

> The phrase lines count from the first downbeat Touch Jockey found. If a track's changes don't land on the lines, trust your ears over the grid, or press **New pair**.

## Your scorecard

When a handoff finishes, a scorecard appears beside the library. A **clean handoff** passes all five checks:

- **Came in on a phrase line**: the new track started on a phrase.
- **Bass swap on a phrase line**: LOWS crossed over on a phrase line, or within a bar of it. Right on the line sounds tightest, and the scorecard tells you when you were a bar out.
- **Basslines together under a bar**: no muddy stretch with both basses playing.
- **Blend over 8 bars or more**: highs and mids came in gradually.
- **Finished before the track ran out**: the old track didn't end mid-blend.

Each check that misses says why, and what to try instead.

## Practice

Do ten handoffs, A to B and back to B to A. Try them at different speeds: one over 8 bars, one over 32. Listen for the moment that sounds best for the bass swap.

## Level up

After **5 clean handoffs**, **Free** bass-swap timing unlocks, and the scorecard offers to switch to it. From then on, a flick lands the moment you make it, so the timing is yours (chapter 6). After **10**, each deck gets a **CUE** button for setting your own starting points.
