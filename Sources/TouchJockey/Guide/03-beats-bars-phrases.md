# Beats, bars and phrases

Dance music is built in neat blocks. Once you can hear them, you'll always know where you are in a track.

## The technique

- A **beat** is one kick drum: the "boom" you nod your head to. House runs at about 120–128 beats a minute.
- A **bar** is 4 beats: **ONE**-two-three-four. The ONE is the strongest.
- A **phrase** is usually **8 bars** (32 beats). Dance tracks change on phrase boundaries: a new sound comes in, the bass drops out, a build starts.

DJs start and change things on phrase boundaries, because that's when the crowd expects something to happen. A new track entering mid-phrase sounds wrong even to people who couldn't say why.

## In Touch Jockey

- The **timeline** draws faint lines on beats, brighter lines on bars, and labelled lines on phrases.
- The deck card shows **Bar** and **beat**. The phone's dots light up on each beat of the bar.
- **Next bar** and **Next phrase** start the incoming track exactly on a boundary. **Next phrase** waits for the phrase line, so it's always in phrase; **Next bar** starts sooner, on the next bar.

## Practice

Play deck A. Count out loud, "ONE-two-three-four", eight times, and listen for the change at the next phrase line on the timeline. Do this until you can predict the change before it happens.
